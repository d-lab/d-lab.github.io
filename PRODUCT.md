# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audiences are:

- **Prospective PhD students and visiting researchers** deciding whether DLab is the right place to study or spend a research visit.
- **Academic collaborators and reviewers** checking what the lab works on, what it has published, and who is involved.
- **Partners and funders** in government, industry, and non-profit organizations looking for research-backed expertise in responsible AI.

Secondary visitors include current members and alumni, students at The University of Queensland, journalists, and readers of the lab's blog.

## Product Purpose

The site presents DLab as a research group: its mission, its people, its publications, and the community around it. Success means a visitor can quickly understand what the lab researches and why, verify that work through publications, see who does it, and find a clear way to join, visit, or collaborate.

The site speaks for a group rather than a single person. It should credit individual members for their work and writing while presenting a coherent shared identity. Group photography and lab stories are a distinctive secondary expression of the lab's culture, not the primary professional story.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, collegial, purposeful, and confident. The lab studies the risks and harms of AI and builds AI for public good, so the substance must stay credible and careful. The presentation should still make a strong first impression, like an organisation that partners with government and industry: a clear mission, visible momentum, and obvious ways to get involved. It should feel like a real group of people working together, led by research, people, and photographs.

The site is a blend of a research website and an organisation website: research-grade content, organisation-grade presentation.

## Voice

- Write as "we" for the lab and name individuals when work or writing is theirs.
- Describe research in plain, specific language a technical non-specialist can follow.
- State facts about funding, venues, and roles precisely. Do not inflate impact.
- Keep calls to action direct: who should get in touch, about what, and how.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI-startup landing-page aesthetics: glowing gradients, blurred colour blobs, glassmorphism, stock imagery, and hype language about "the future of AI". Avoid a corporate tone that erases the people in the lab. Equally, avoid a bare academic page or a bland university template that undersells the group: a plain list of links with no imagery, figures, or invitation to engage. Do not reduce the lab to a logo wall of sponsors, and do not let decorative effects compete with publications or people.

## Design Principles

- Make a strong first impression with real people and a clear mission.
- Make the research agenda understandable before asking visitors to explore.
- Show momentum with real evidence, such as recent publications and news, never invented or inflated.
- Show the people behind the work and credit them by name.
- Make every route to joining, visiting, or collaborating easy to find and act on.
- Prefer clear hierarchy and consistent language over decorative novelty.
- Keep content easy for any lab member to update without touching layout code.
- Keep interactions fast, predictable, and reversible.
- Polish the system as a whole rather than perfecting isolated pages.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across desktop and mobile. Support keyboard navigation, screen readers, visible focus, 44px touch targets, sufficient colour contrast, reduced-motion preferences, robust light and dark themes, semantic landmarks and headings, meaningful image alternative text, and layouts that remain usable when text is enlarged.

The lab's audience is international. Prefer plain English, avoid unexplained local acronyms, and make application requirements and funding amounts unambiguous.
